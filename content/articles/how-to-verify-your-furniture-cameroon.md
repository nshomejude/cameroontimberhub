Buying furniture or raw timber in Cameroon rarely comes with a certificate, and that is not unusual for a domestic market built on relationships rather than paperwork. It does mean the burden of verification sits more with the buyer's own judgement. None of the checks below need special equipment or expertise — they are things anyone can do standing in a workshop or timber yard.

## Ask about origin, and listen for consistency, not detail

You are unlikely to get a harvest permit number from a small carpenter, and that alone is not a red flag — it is simply not how this market usually works. What is worth listening for is whether the answer is consistent and specific: the same source named the same way if you ask again later in the conversation, versus a vague or shifting answer. A seller who has bought from the same yard or source for years and can describe it — where it is, roughly how they came to deal with them — is showing you a real relationship. A seller who cannot say where their timber came from at all, or gives a different answer to the same question twice, is telling you something too.

See [Why buy legal Cameroon wood](insights:why-buy-legal-cameroon-wood) for why this matters beyond the immediate transaction.

## Check the wood itself for signs of proper drying

This is the check most within a non-specialist's reach. Timber or furniture made from wood that has not dried sufficiently tends to show specific signs: it feels heavier than expected for its size, joints that should be tight show small gaps, and flat surfaces are not quite flat — a slight cup or twist visible when you sight along the edge. Freshly cut or poorly dried wood can also have a faint green or "wet" smell that properly seasoned timber does not.

None of these checks are definitive on their own, but together they are a reasonable proxy for whether the wood had time to season before it was worked — a step covered in more depth in [From forest to furniture](insights:from-forest-to-furniture).

## Inspect joinery, not just the surface finish

A good finish can hide mediocre joinery, and a plain finish can sit on top of excellent joinery — the two are not the same thing. Look at how pieces actually connect: are joints tight with no visible gaps, do drawers and doors sit flush and move smoothly, is there any wobble when you apply gentle pressure to a corner. Turn a piece over or open it up where practical — the underside and interior of a well-made piece usually look as considered as the visible surfaces; a rushed piece often shows its shortcuts exactly where the buyer isn't expected to look.

## Ask about species by name, and cross-check the answer

Ask which species was used, and if the seller names one, compare what you are told against what that species should look and feel like — colour, grain pattern, relative weight. [Know your wood](insights:know-your-wood-cameroon) gives a working description of common species for exactly this purpose. A mismatch does not automatically mean deception; species can be misidentified honestly, especially by someone further down the chain from the original sawyer. But a seller who cannot explain the mismatch when you point it out, or who changes their answer, is worth being more cautious with.

## Use track record as your strongest signal

The single best predictor of a good outcome in this market is not any individual check — it is whether the carpenter or yard has an established, checkable track record: work you can see that they made before, other customers who can vouch for them, a location they have operated from for years rather than a one-off pop-up. This is also the reasoning behind [the Meet the Maker series](insights:meet-the-maker-series) — putting names and track records behind the general advice to "find someone you trust."

## Get the basics in writing, even informally

A simple note — species agreed, dimensions, price, delivery timeline, what happens if something is wrong when delivered — protects both sides and costs nothing to produce. It will not read like an export contract, and it does not need to. It just needs to exist, so that "what we agreed" is not a matter of two people's memory a month later.
